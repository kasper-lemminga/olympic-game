1. Introduction

The Olympic Village Planner is an interactive urban simulation tool designed to explore the balance between housing, industry, recreation, and green space. It functions as a "living blueprint," where you act as the zoning authority, painting designations onto a grid, while the simulation fills those zones with buildings, trees, and activity based on a set of dynamic rules.

2. Interface Overview

The interface is divided into four main sections:

  • Top Bar: Current year, manual, day/night mode, layout files, image export and settings.
  • Left Sidebar: Simulation controls, zoning palette, and planning rules.
  • Center Canvas: The interactive map where you paint and observe the simulation.
  • Right Sidebar: Composition breakdown and detailed configuration sliders.

3. Controls & Navigation

  • Left Click: Paint with the selected brush (Draw Mode) / open the Plot Info of a plot (Simulation Mode).
  • Click & Drag: Paint continuously.
  • Shift + Drag: Fill a rectangle with the selected brush.
  • Right Click + Drag: Pan the view.
  • Scroll Wheel: Zoom In / Out.

4. Getting Started

To begin using the planner:

  1. Select a Tool: Click on a zone type in the Zoning Palette (Left Sidebar).
  2. Paint: Click and drag on the map to apply the zone.
  3. Run: Click the Start Simulation button in the Status card.
  4. Observe: Watch as plots spawn, age, and evolve based on your design and the active rules.

5. Simulation Controls

Located in the Status card on the top left:

  • START SIMULATION / STOP & EDIT ZONES: Switches between Draw Mode (painting zones) and Simulation Mode. Stopping keeps everything that has grown so far, so you can adjust the zoning and continue.
  • Play: Resumes the simulation.
  • Pause: Freezes time. Useful for inspecting specific details or taking screenshots.
  • Reset: Starts over from the year 2025 with an empty grid.
Reset also clears the painted zoning, paths and removed tiles. Use Save Layout first, and Load Layout afterwards to run the same plan again.

One simulation step equals one year.

6. View Modes & Visuals

The toolbar floating on the top-left of the map provides several visualization options:

  • Suitability Metrics: Displays the suitability score of each plot as a number (see section 10).
  • Heatmap: Darkens plots according to how far their score lies above the base score of their type. Dark areas are strong clusters of the same use.
  • Shadows: Turns the shadows of buildings and trees on/off.
  • Objects: Hides/Shows the 3D isometric buildings and trees.
  • Pixel Mode: Simplify the view into a retro, pixelated aesthetic.
  • Reset View: Centers the camera on the grid.

Tab Navigation:

  • Grid View: The standard isometric map.
  • Analytics: A historical timeline graph and detailed KPI dashboard.

Plot Info: In Simulation Mode, click a plot to see its type, zone, position, merge status, paths and suitability score. Click it again (or click outside the grid) to close.

7. The Zoning Palette

Select these brushes to paint on the grid. A zone is a designation, not a building: it makes a certain use more likely, and the simulation decides what actually appears.

  • NEUTRAL: No preference. Any use can appear. Also use it to clear a painted zone.
  • GREEN: Parks, gardens and wilderness.
  • SPORT: Recreational facilities (courts, fields).
  • WORKSHOP: Light industrial/production spaces.
  • FARMING: Agricultural plots (greenhouses, fields).
  • SKELETON (S): Road infrastructure. Nothing is built here.
  • AQUEDUCT: Historic water infrastructure (protected). Nothing is built here.
  • EXISTING HOUSING: Places existing buildings (context). These are permanent structures that influence neighbors but don't change.
  • NONE: Excluded zone. Plots stay empty and do not take part in the simulation.
  • PATH: Draws pedestrian paths along the edges of tiles. Shift + Click to remove.
  • FREEZE (snowflake): Locks plots in place so they are unaffected by the simulation. See section 8 below.
  • BOARD EDITOR (red): Changes the shape of the board itself. Click to add a tile back, Shift + Click (or Shift + Drag) to remove tiles.

8. Freeze Tool

The Freeze Tool (snowflake icon) allows you to lock specific plots on the grid. Frozen plots are completely excluded from the simulation: they will not age, decay, spawn new buildings, change type, or participate in merges. This is useful for preserving a specific layout or testing how the simulation behaves around fixed elements.

  • Freeze: Select the Freeze tool, then click and drag a rectangle over the plots you want to lock. A blue overlay will appear on frozen tiles.
  • Unfreeze: Hold Shift while clicking and dragging to remove the freeze from a selection of tiles.
The Freeze tool is only available in Draw Mode. It automatically deactivates when you switch to Simulation Mode.

9. Planning Constraints (The Rules)

These toggles in the Left Sidebar are the hard rules of the game. Turning them on/off changes how the village evolves. Hover over a rule to highlight on the map where it applies; right-click a rule to keep that highlight pinned.

  • Workshop Noise Buffer: Workshops cannot appear within 2 tiles of Existing Housing.
  • Sport Noise Buffer: Sports facilities cannot appear directly next to Existing Housing.
  • Threshold Zone: Buildings position and orient themselves within their plot in relation to Skeleton roads and paths.
  • Build Threshold Zone: A Farming plot places its greenhouse on the side facing away from adjacent Existing Housing. If that is impossible, no farm appears there.
  • Aqueduct Zone: Only Green plots can appear next to the historic Aqueduct.
  • Tree Donation to Green Type: Sport, Farming and Workshop plots "donate" their tree to an adjacent Green plot, making parks denser.
  • Door to Plot: New plots can create doors on adjacent Existing Buildings, graphically linking new development to old (visual only).
  • Roof Gardens: Allows Existing Buildings to grow a garden on their roof.
  • Green Zone Compliance: Workshops in Green Zones get a green roof and a decorative tree to blend in.
  • Plot Merging: Two adjacent plots of the same type can merge into one larger plot once they are old enough.
  • Green Lung: 60% of the village surface must remain unpaved. Once the Paved Surface reaches 40%, only Green and Farming plots can appear until it drops below the limit again.

10. How the Simulation Decides (Suitability)

This is the engine behind everything you see. Every year, each plot that takes part in the simulation is evaluated on its own. Nothing is planned from above: the pattern emerges from many small local decisions, each of them a roll of the dice with odds that depend on the location.

An empty plot: what could appear here?

For each of the four uses (Green, Farming, Sport, Workshop) the simulation adds up a development chance:

  • Market Demand: the base chance of that use (default 1%).
  • + Zoning Compliance: added when the plot lies in the zone of that use (default 20%). Farming also gets half of this bonus in a Green zone.
  • + Proximity Bonus: added for each of the 8 surrounding plots that already has the same use (default 10% per neighbor). This is what makes uses cluster.
  • + Infrastructure Weight: added for Workshops next to a Skeleton road (default 10%).

The planning rules of section 9 work as a veto: if a rule forbids a use on this plot, its chance is zero no matter how high the sum is. The four uses are then tried in random order, and the first one that wins its roll is built. If none wins, the plot stays empty for another year.

Example. An empty plot in a Green zone with two Green neighbors has a chance of roughly 1% + 20% + 2 × 10% = 41% per year to become Green. The same plot has only a 1% chance to become a Workshop. Zoning steers, but never fully dictates.

A developed plot: does it stay?

  • Attrition: every year a plot has a small chance to fall out of use (Attrition Rate, default 3%). It then decays and is empty again one year later.
  • Re-evaluation: once a plot is older than the Maturity Age (default 10 years, the length of a lease cycle) it has a 10% chance per year to be reconsidered. The same sum as above is made again, and if another use wins, the plot changes. A plot that no longer fits its surroundings is therefore replaced over time, while a plot in a strong cluster tends to be confirmed.
  • Merging: two neighboring plots of the same use that are both older than the Merge Age can merge (Merge Chance per year).

The suitability score

The number shown by Suitability Metrics, the Heatmap and the Plot Info is a reading of how well a plot is embedded in its surroundings:

score = base score of its use + bonus for each of the 8 neighbors with the same use

  • Base score: set per use with the Base Score sliders (default 1.0).
  • Neighbor bonus: Green +0.25, Sport +0.20, Workshop +0.15, Farming +0.15 per neighbor.

With the default settings a lonely plot scores 1.0 and a Green plot completely surrounded by Green scores 3.0. Empty plots have no score.

The score measures clustering, it does not cause it. The decisions are made by the development chance above. Both use the same ingredient (same-use neighbors), so a high score marks a place where that use is also most likely to be confirmed or to spread.

11. Configuration (Right Sidebar)

Fine-tune the behavior of the simulation engine. Section 10 explains how these values work together.

Stability Parameters (Base Scores)

  • Green / Farm / Sport / Work: The starting value of the suitability score for each use. This changes what the Suitability Metrics and Heatmap show; it does not change how fast plots decay or are replaced.

Market Demand

Controls the "birth rate" of new plots.

  • Green Space / Agriculture / Sports / Production: The base chance per year for that use to appear on an empty plot.

Simulation Constants

  • Time Scale: Speed of the simulation (time per year).
  • Zoning Compliance: How strongly a zone attracts its own use (Lower = more chaotic/organic mixing).
  • Proximity Bonus: How much each neighbor of the same use raises the chance (Higher = stronger clusters).
  • Infrastructure Weight: Extra chance for Workshops next to Skeleton roads.
  • Attrition Rate: Chance per year that a plot falls out of use (Turnover rate).
  • Maturity Age: How many years a plot stays untouched before it can be re-evaluated.
  • Merge Age & Chance: How old two neighboring plots must be before they can merge into one double plot, and the chance per year that they do.
  • Door Chance: Chance that a new plot creates a door on an adjacent existing building.
  • Topping Chance: Chance per year that an existing building gets a roof garden.

Context Layer

Allows you to upload an underlay image (e.g., a real site plan) to trace over. You can adjust its Opacity, Position, and Scale to match the grid.

Settings (Wrench Icon, Top Bar)

  • Grid Size: Number of columns and rows. Changing it restarts the simulation.
  • Simulation Seed: A specific number that initializes the random generator. Using the same seed will produce the exact same sequence of events. Changing it restarts the simulation.
  • Spatial Seed Mode: When enabled, plots generate randomness based on their fixed coordinates rather than a global sequence.
    • Enabled (Spatial Hashing): Allows you to isolate parameter tweaks (A/B testing) without causing a "butterfly effect" across the entire map.
    • Disabled (Butterfly Effect): The village evolves organically and chaotically; a single change causes the timeline to fracture for all subsequent events.
  • Level of Detail: Simplifies the drawing when zoomed out, for better performance on large grids.
  • Pixel Detail Factor: How coarse the pixels of Pixel Mode are.
  • Data Export: See section 13.

12. Analytics & KPIs

Monitor the health of your neighborhood in the Analytics tab:

  • Composition Timeline: The share of Green, Farming, Sport, Workshop and empty plots through the years.
  • Current Trees / Total Planted: Trees standing now, and all trees planted during the run.
  • Participating Plots: Plots that take part in the simulation. Removed tiles, NONE zones, Skeleton, Aqueduct and Existing Housing are excluded.
  • Paved Surface: Percentage of hardscape (Workshops, Sport, Existing Buildings, Skeleton roads and paths).
  • Bio-Permeable: Percentage of land that absorbs water (Green, Farming, Aqueduct and empty plots, minus the paths on them).
  • Avg. Building Age: Average age of all developed plots. A high age indicates a stable, mature neighborhood.
  • Door Count: Number of doors created on existing buildings.
  • Diversity Score: Measures the mix of Green, Farming, Sport and Workshop. 100 means a perfect equal mix; 0 means a monoculture.
  • % Merged: How many plots of each use have merged into larger plots.

13. Saving & Exporting

  • Save/Load Layout: Saves your Zoning Map (zones, paths and the shape of the board) to a JSON file so you can continue working later. A layout can only be loaded into a grid of the same size.
  • Export Image (Camera Icon): Takes a high-resolution screenshot.
    • Resolution: 1x up to 10x (A0 Poster Quality), or a 4-tile A0 export in 4 files.
    • Format: JPG or PNG.
    • Scope: "Current View" captures what you see; "Entire Map" captures the whole grid, even parts off-screen.
  • Export .JSON (Settings): Exports the 3D data structure for import into Blender 3D for professional rendering.
